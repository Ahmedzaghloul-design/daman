## The problem

- Since 1 January 2026 the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) is
  in its definitive period (F2). It covers cement, iron and steel, aluminium,
  fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen (F1).
- Your EU buyer declares the emissions embedded in what you sell them and
  buys CBAM certificates for them (F10, F12). They rely on data from you,
  the producer (F10, F15).
- Without verified data from your plant, the buyer can use the EU's default
  values. Defaults carry a mark-up: +10% in 2026, +20% in 2027 and +30% from
  2028 for cement, iron and steel, aluminium and hydrogen (F3), so buying
  from you gets more expensive on paper.

## What Daman does

1. Your team photographs the plant's handwritten Arabic fuel and energy
   receipts in the Daman app, or sends them on WhatsApp.
2. Daman reads each receipt, flags anything unreadable or unusual for your
   production, and holds it until one of your reviewers approves it.
3. Approved entries become a per-product emissions report (direct and
   indirect, F14), ready for your verifier and for sharing with EU buyers
   through the CBAM Registry's operator module (O3CI, F13).

## What a pilot looks like

- One installation, one reporting period, about 2 to 4 weeks.
- Your team: one owner, one or two reviewers, one person who sends receipts.
- You receive: the emissions report for your products, the share backed by
  your real data, and a comparison with the EU default values.
- [Team to decide: price of the pilot, e.g. free.] Daman is a student
  project at The American University in Cairo. Nothing is shared with anyone
  without your written approval.

## What Daman is not

- Not a CBAM verifier and not legal advice. Your buyer stays responsible for
  their declaration (F15); an accredited verifier checks actual data.
- Emission factors in the current version are illustrative and must be
  replaced with the factors agreed with your verifier before real use.
